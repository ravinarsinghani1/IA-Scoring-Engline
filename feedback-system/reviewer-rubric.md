# Reviewer Rubric — how to review any Form A (internal SOP)

Apply this to every candidate so feedback is consistent. The goal is not to
grade the proposal — it is to identify what must change **now** so the finished
exploration can reach the top band of all five criteria. Output goes into
[`one-page-feedback-template.md`](one-page-feedback-template.md).

## 0. Read the whole Form A first
Note: candidate name, **course (AA/AI)**, **level (SL/HL)**, working title,
proposed RQ, planned mathematics, data source, personal-meaning statement.

## 1. The five criteria — what "top band" needs
The IA is marked /20. A "7/7" exploration reaches the top band of *all five*.

| Crit | Max | Top-band test to apply to the proposal |
|---|---|---|
| A Presentation | 4 | Can this be told as intro→aim→conclusion, concise, figures in-body? |
| B Communication | 4 | Will it force proper notation, defined variables/units, consistency? |
| C Personal engagement | 3 | Is there **genuine ownership** — own data, own question, own angle? Not effort/length. |
| D Reflection | 3 | Is there room for **critical reflection threaded throughout** (limitations, comparing approaches), not just a closing paragraph? |
| E Use of mathematics | 6 | Is the maths **relevant, correct, and commensurate with the course/level**, with real depth (not trivial, not off-syllabus)? |

C is almost always the differentiator, not E. Push personalization hard.

## 2. The seven standard checks (run every time)

1. **Scope — specific vs category.** Reject "a tradition / a whole class of
   objects." Force a *named, specific* artifact/dataset the maths acts on.
2. **RQ — measurable vs descriptive.** "Differences and similarities",
   "explore", "how is maths used in…" ⇒ descriptive ⇒ low. Rewrite as a
   question answered by *computed quantities*; encourage a testable hypothesis.
3. **Mathematical depth — commensurate with level.**
   - **AA** wants analytic/pure work: algebra, trigonometry, functions,
     **calculus**, coordinate geometry, and deductive **reasoning/proof**.
   - **AI** wants **modelling with real data**, statistics, and interpretation
     in context.
   - **HL** requires *sophistication and rigour* for E5–6; **SL does not**
     (correctness is decisive at SL).
   - Flag if planned maths is **too elementary** (areas of squares, basic
     angle-counting, plugging into one formula) or **off-syllabus**.
4. **Off-syllabus traps to catch** (esp. SL): transformation matrices, formal
   group/wallpaper-symmetry theory, fractal/box-counting dimension, Markov
   chains (AI HL only), differential equations (AA HL only). Descriptive
   symmetry is fine as framing; formal group theory is not.
5. **Personal engagement — primary material.** Is the student using their own
   photos/measurements/data, or generic internet material? Steer to primary.
6. **Reflection — threaded vs end-only.** If the plan saves "evaluate
   limitations" for the last step, prescribe reflection *in every section*, and
   name the specific limitations this topic invites.
7. **Cliché / integrity traps.** Golden-ratio hunting, BMI/obesity, "is a
   dice/roulette fair", correlation-as-causation, φ or Fibonacci "found"
   everywhere. Allow only if tested *honestly* (state uncertainty; be willing to
   conclude the pattern is absent).

## 3. Verdict (pick one for the one-pager)
- **Approved** — proceed as proposed (rare at Form A).
- **Approved with revisions** — good topic; fixes listed must be done before
  starting. (Most common.)
- **Needs rework** — core problem (un-measurable, off-syllabus, un-personalizable,
  or maths far too thin) must be resolved and re-submitted.

## 4. Fill the template
- **What's working:** 2–3 genuine, specific strengths (name the actual content).
- **Priority revisions:** 3–5 numbered fixes, each tied to a criterion, each a
  concrete action on *this* student's topic — never generic advice.
- **Checklist:** keep the A–E tick-boxes; specialise a few items to the topic.
- **Next step:** ONE thing the student must reply with to move forward.
- Keep the whole thing to **one page**. Warm, direct, specific.
